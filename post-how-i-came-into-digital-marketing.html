How I Came Into Digital Marketing

A short story about how a post-college side hustle in 2018 — affiliate marketing to European audiences — quietly became the foundation of everything I build today.

Digital MarketingAffiliateOrigin Story

People assume I started with code. I did not. I started with traffic — with the strange, addictive craft of moving strangers from one place on the internet to another.

The 2018 decision

In 2018, right after college, my friend Junayed and I decided we needed to earn something online — at minimum, enough to cover our own pocket money. We enrolled in a small local course together. The irony is that Junayed himself was teaching part of it. We were both students and, in a way, both experimenting.

Out-earning the instructor

Within a few months the two of us were earning more than the instructor who taught the course. Our channel was affiliate marketing, and our audience was in Europe. That was the first time I understood that the internet does not reward credentials — it rewards attention, aimed carefully.

What traffic actually taught me

From there I went deep. Facebook Ads. Taboola. Native. Landing-page split tests. Reading heatmaps at 2 a.m. Rewriting a single headline seven times because the CTR was 0.4% and I knew it could be 0.9%. Marketing taught me a way of thinking that engineering later refined: measure the thing, change one variable, measure again.

Why this still shapes how I build Shopify stores

Every Shopify store I build now is filtered through that lens. A theme is not decoration — it is a funnel. A product page is not a spec sheet — it is an argument. When I optimise a Liquid template, I am really optimising a conversation between the brand and someone who has three seconds to care.

The marketing came first. The code came to serve it. That order, I think, is the reason clients keep me around.
