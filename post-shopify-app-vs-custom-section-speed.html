Do Shopify Apps Slow Down Your Store? App vs Custom Section

Short answer: yes, apps can slow your store, because most of them load extra scripts on every page. A custom Liquid section usually does not, because it lives inside your theme. The right choice is not "never use apps" — it is knowing which job belongs to an app, and which belongs to a section.

ShopifySpeedCustom SectionsApps

Why apps slow a store down

An app is not just a button in your admin. When a shopper opens your store, many apps add their own JavaScript, CSS, fonts, or calls to an outside server. One good app, used in the right place, may barely matter. Five or ten apps, all loading on every page whether they are needed or not, add up fast — and the product page, the page that has to sell, is usually where it hurts most.

That is also why "my store feels slow" is hard to diagnose from the outside. The theme may be fine. The apps may be the weight.

App vs custom section, side by side

SpeedOften slower, extra scripts and outside requestsUsually faster, code lives in the theme

Data and structureStored in the app, harder to moveCan use Metafields and Metaobjects, so content stays with the store

The rule I use with clients is simple: if the feature is mainly something a shopper sees, build it as a section. If the feature needs a service behind the scenes, use an app — and only that app, not three apps doing the same job.

How to check if an app is the problem

  1. Note your Shopify speed score and run a Lighthouse or PageSpeed test on a product page.
  2. Duplicate your theme, remove or disable the suspect app on the duplicate, and test again. Never test by breaking the live theme.
  3. Check whether the app loads on every page, or only on the pages that need it. An app that loads everywhere for a feature used on one page is a clear candidate to replace.
  4. If removing it clearly helps, ask whether that feature can be rebuilt as a custom section, a Metafield, or a Shopify Flow automation instead.

I do not quote a fixed number of seconds for "one app = X slower", because it is not true. A light, well-built app and a heavy, poorly built app are different things. Test your own store, before and after, and let that decide.

What I build instead, in real client work

This is the work I do most: custom sections and templates in Liquid, structured with Metafields and Metaobjects, so a merchant can edit content in the theme editor without touching code, and without adding an app for every block.

Two examples from my own work: Ready Prep Go, an Irish brand built from scratch on Shopify through Scopioe, and a custom product template for Getsure Goods, a dropshipping store and a separate client, not a Coalition client project. In both cases the point was the same — a clean, editable storefront, not a stack of apps doing layout work that Liquid can do natively. At Coalition Technologies (July 2025 – June 2026) the same pattern applied: custom sections, headers, and Metafield/Metaobject work on live client stores, plus WCAG 2.1 accessibility fixes.

So, app or custom section?

Use an app when you need a backend, a service, or logic Liquid cannot do well. Use a custom section when you need speed, control, and a product page that stays fast as you grow. And audit the apps you already have — most slow stores I see are not slow because of one bad app, but because of five good apps that were never removed when the store outgrew them.
