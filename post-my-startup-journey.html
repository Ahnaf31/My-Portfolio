My Startup Journey: From Side Hustles to Scopioe

The short version: I did not start with a startup. I started with side hustles, learned to build because I had to, tried to run my own brands, failed at one of them for a year, and came out of it a much better builder for other people's businesses.

StartupShopifyScopioeCareer

The short answer first

If you only want the journey in one paragraph, here it is: affiliate marketing taught me how traffic and landing pages actually work. Freelance Shopify work taught me how to ship for real clients. A job in digital marketing taught me how agencies think. Then, with friends, I co-founded Scopioe — and alongside it, I tried running my own e-commerce brands in Bangladesh. One of those ventures, an organic products startup under Shahnaf & Co, ran for about a year and did not become commercially successful. That failure is not a footnote. It is the reason I build stores the way I do now.

It started as a side hustle, not a plan

After college, I got into affiliate marketing — mostly to European audiences. Every campaign needed its own landing page, its own product page, its own funnel. Nobody was going to build those for me, so I built them myself. HTML, CSS and JavaScript stopped being theory and became tools I used every day.

From 2019 to 2023 I did the same thing on Fiverr and with Adcombo: Shopify development and affiliate marketing, for international clients. That is where I learned the most important freelance lesson early — a client does not pay for code, they pay for a store that sells, loads fast, and does not break when they edit it.

Aggressive ROI, and then a decision

In early 2023 I worked as a Digital Marketing Executive at Aggressive ROI, from January to June. It gave me the agency side of the picture: briefs, deadlines, clients who change their minds, work that has to survive contact with a real business.

After that, instead of only working for other people, I started building with friends. That became Scopioe — a digital solutions agency for website development, UI/UX design and mobile applications. I am a co-founder there, and larger client engagements still go through Scopioe today.

My own brands: AnimaxBd, and the organic year

Between agency work and client work, I also ran my own e-commerce in the local market — print-on-demand and dropshipping style. That meant everything at once: product sourcing, the website, social media pages, and content. No team to hand things to. If a product page was slow, it was my page. If a post did not land, it was my post.

Two of those projects tell the story best:

  • AnimaxBd — a print-on-demand project for the anime-lover community in Bangladesh. It taught me how much a niche audience matters: when people recognise themselves in the product, marketing gets easier.
  • Shahnaf & Co — the plan was to build a startup from scratch around organic products. Furrybari was the animals and organic side of it, and Tawaqqal Farm sat under Shahnaf & Co. I tried this for about a year. It did not succeed.

I am not going to dress that year up. Sourcing organic products, keeping quality consistent, managing a farm-linked brand, running pages and content, and trying to make the numbers work — all at the same time — is a different job from building a website. I learned that building the store is the easy part. The hard part is the business around it: who exactly is buying, why they come back, and what it costs to serve them properly.

What that failed year changed about my client work

That is the part clients actually benefit from now. When I build a Shopify store today, I am not just thinking about sections and templates. I am thinking about:

  • Can the merchant edit this without breaking it? — because I have been the merchant editing at midnight.
  • Is the product story clear in five seconds? — because a beautiful homepage that confuses people does not sell.
  • Does the structure scale? — Metafields and Metaobjects, clean JSON templates, and Shopify Flow automations are not fancy extras. They are what keeps a growing catalogue from turning into chaos.
  • Can everyone use it? — WCAG 2.1 accessibility work matters because real customers include real people with real needs, not just ideal buyers.

That mindset carried straight into my first real Liquid project — Ready Prep Go, an Irish restaurant brand, built from scratch through Scopioe — and later into remote front-end work at Coalition Technologies (July 2025 – June 2026), where I shipped custom sections, headers, and Metafield/Metaobject work for international client stores.

So, what is the startup story, really?

It is not a straight line from idea to success. It is: learn to build by necessity, learn marketing by doing it, co-found an agency with friends, try your own brands, fail honestly at one of them, and keep the lessons. Scopioe is still the vehicle for bigger work. Shopify is still the craft. And the failed year is still the cheapest MBA I will ever get — I just paid for it with time instead of tuition.

Never say why me? — life is unfair by design, and that's where the work begins.
